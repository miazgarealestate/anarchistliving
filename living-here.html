The commute

Closer than
people assume

The mountain sits about 15 kilometres east of Osoyoos, and the drive down for groceries, errands, or a coffee in town takes 15 to 20 minutes. It's a real highway commute, not a rough mountain track — sections run 100 km/h, some stretches drop to 80, and the tightest hairpin switchbacks slow to around 30 km/h. Those same switchbacks come with some of the best views on the drive.

Traffic is light — almost nonexistent in winter, a little heavier during peak summer — but the actual commute time rarely changes much. The only real slowdown is getting stuck behind a truck in a line of cars, which isn't a common occurrence.

Winter driving takes more attention, and the highway crews keep the road well maintained, along with the side streets — Sasquatch, Eagle, Cariboo, Mobier, and the others. Your job is really just plowing your own driveway to the main road. The higher up the mountain you are, the more likely you are to catch cloud cover — it can turn into genuine fog with limited visibility. But the flip side is a real perk: on many winter days there's a temperature inversion, where the valley below sits under cloud or even rain while Anarchist Mountain sits above it in blue sky and sun.

Oliver, with its hospital, is a bit further past Osoyoos. Most day-to-day needs are handled in the 15-20 minute window down to Osoyoos.

The valley below can be socked in with cloud — while up here, it's blue sky.

Connectivity

No fiber yet.
Doesn't matter much.

There's no fiber optic service on Anarchist Mountain as of today. That used to be a real dealbreaker for remote workers looking at rural acreage — it isn't anymore.

What Pat uses

Starlink

Satellite internet. Fast and very reliable in day-to-day use — this is what handles video calls, streaming, and everything else without issue.

Cell-tower based

Telus & Bell

Neighbors using these carriers ping off cell towers rather than fiber, and report the service works well for calls, data, and home internet setups.

More variable

Rogers & Fido

Same cell-tower dependence, but coverage tends to be spottier for these carriers specifically — worth testing before you commit if this is your provider.

Bottom line: if you're moving up here to work remotely, Starlink is the practical answer most residents have already landed on.

Who lives here

A retiree community
that's shifting younger

Anarchist Mountain has historically skewed toward an older crowd — mostly retirees at a later stage of life who came up for the quiet, the acreage, and the views. That's still a big part of the community.

What's changing is the number of younger families and couples moving up. There are now roughly a couple dozen families with children living on the mountain, and that number has been growing. It's a real shift from what the mountain looked like even a few years ago.

It's a tight-knit community with regular seasonal gatherings — a mountain park with a stage where local bands play live music, potluck dinners, barbecues, and bonfires throughout the year. Once the snow arrives at the end of November, it tends to stay frozen on top of the mountain, turning the whole place crystalline white — genuinely beautiful, as the photos below from right on the mountain show.

There's also a strong mix of trades among residents — electricians, plumbers, general contractors — the kind of neighborly, helpful community where people genuinely look out for each other. At the same time, the acreages give real elbow room between properties, so it's private enough that people can do their own thing. It's quiet, it's chill, and it's friendly — people walk, wave, and say hi to one another. Nothing like the anonymity of the city.

Where locals actually go

Past Sidley Mountain,
the Kettle River

Keep heading past Anarchist Mountain toward Rock Creek and you hit Sidley Mountain — the next mountain over, close enough that kids from both share the same school bus route.

Rock Creek is where the Kettle River runs, and it's a genuine local recreation spot rather than a tourist destination — fishing, river tubing on hot summer days, swimming holes, and the odd bridge jump for the braver crowd. It's not heavily marketed anywhere, which is part of the appeal — it's the kind of place you find out about from a neighbor, not a travel blog.

Growing here

The greenhouse
does surprisingly well

Between the elevation and the sun, the growing conditions up here are better than people expect. Even at the top of the mountain, vegetables, flowers, and beautiful vegetation grow well — as long as you put the care into it. Tomatoes, peppers, corn, snow peas, lettuce, strawberries, and raspberries all do well in a greenhouse setup, and several neighbors grow excellent rhubarb.

This year's new experiment is giant pumpkins — the plants have taken off and are thriving so far. It's one of those small things that says a lot about what's possible up here with a bit of attention and effort.

Sharing the mountain

You're not the only
ones living up here

Wildlife is a real part of life on Anarchist Mountain — black bears, coyotes, and both black-tail and white-tail deer are regular sightings. Flocks of wild turkeys move through the properties; you'll hear them gobbling before you see them running around in groups.

Smaller residents show up too — garter snakes, blue-tailed skinks, and at least one resident badger that's been spotted on the mountain. And on quiet evenings, it's not unusual to hear cattle lowing in the distance from the ranches and farmland over on Sidley Mountain.

Deer and wild turkeys are a daily sight on the mountain — crossing the highway, wandering the garden, even wandering right up to the window.

Beyond the property line

Fall here is
something else

Fall on the mountain is genuinely gorgeous — golden larches in the distance against rolling grassy hills. It's the kind of view that's easy to take for granted once you live here, and hard to believe the first time you see it.

There are ponds and backcountry trails that locals know well, used for hiking, exploring the back roads, ATVing, and horseback riding. The area has a real working-land character too — several ranches and farms around the mountain, some raising livestock, others growing produce.

Winter recreation

Mount Baldy —
35 minutes away

For skiing and snowboarding, Mount Baldy is about 30-35 minutes from Anarchist Mountain — genuinely beautiful terrain, real snow, and none of the lift lines or crowds you'd find at bigger resorts. Rime ice builds up on the lift towers and dead-standing trees on the ridge into these striking frozen "ghost tree" shapes, and the views down into the valley from the top are some of the best in the region.
